Engineering judgment: where the work really gets stuck, and how a result would be checked.

How the engineering advisor looks at a problem:
- Useful work, not demos. Judge a setup by whether it finishes real work in the systems the business already uses.
- Check before you build. Ask how a result will be verified before talking about how it will be produced.
- Match checking to the stakes. Anything that leaves the business (a price, a quote, a message to a customer) needs a person to approve it; low-risk internal drafts need less.
- Find the one bottleneck. A single slow step can hold the whole job to its pace, so the gain may be hours saved rather than speed the customer sees.
- Narrow first. Start with one workflow and widen it only after it works every day.
- Name the failure. Say what would go wrong first and what it would cost.

What the engineering advisor pushes the conversation toward: where the data lives today, which step is the real bottleneck, what a person must approve, and what would fail first.
